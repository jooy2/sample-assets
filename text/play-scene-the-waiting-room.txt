THE WAITING ROOM

A play in one act
Scene 3


CHARACTERS

    NKECHI ADEYEMI     harbourmaster, forties
    TOBIAS RENN        a passenger, thirties
    ADA HALVORSEN      a passenger, eighties
    A VOICE            on the tannoy, unseen


SETTING

The waiting room of a small ferry terminal. Sixteen seats in two rows facing a
window that occupies most of the upstage wall. Beyond it, nothing: fog, lit
grey. A door stage left to the pontoon. A hatch stage right, closed.

A clock above the hatch reads 16.40.

Rain, intermittently, on the roof.


                                    *


        (ADA is seated, centre of the front row, a bag on the seat
        beside her. She has been there some time. TOBIAS stands at
        the window with his back to the room, phone in hand, holding
        it at various heights.)

TOBIAS: There's no signal in here.

        (ADA does not respond.)

TOBIAS: Sorry — is there? Signal. Anywhere.

ADA:    By the door.

TOBIAS: Right. Thank you.

        (He goes to the door, holds the phone up, waits. Nothing.
        He comes back.)

TOBIAS: Nothing by the door.

ADA:    No. Not today.

        (Pause. TOBIAS sits, two seats from ADA. He gets up almost
        immediately and goes back to the window.)

TOBIAS: Can you see anything?

ADA:    No.

TOBIAS: I mean — could you? If it was clear. Could you see the other side?

ADA:    Yes.

TOBIAS: How far is it?

ADA:    Eleven minutes.

TOBIAS: No, I meant — in distance.

ADA:    So did I.

        (TOBIAS looks at her. She does not look back. He returns to
        the window.)


TANNOY: (a click, then) The seventeen-oh-five sailing to Halloway Bank is
        subject to delay. Further information will follow.

        (Click. Silence.)

TOBIAS: That's the third one of those.

ADA:    Fourth.

TOBIAS: Fourth. Right.

        (He sits. Properly, this time, in the back row.)

TOBIAS: Does "subject to delay" mean cancelled?

ADA:    Not yet.

TOBIAS: When does it mean cancelled?

ADA:    When she comes out and says so.

        (TOBIAS looks at the hatch.)

TOBIAS: She's in there?

ADA:    She's in there.

TOBIAS: Why doesn't she just ---

ADA:    Because she doesn't know yet.

        (Pause.)

TOBIAS: How do you know she doesn't know?

ADA:    Because if she knew she'd have come out.

        (TOBIAS considers this. It does not satisfy him.)


        (The hatch opens. NKECHI, behind it, in a coat, a radio in
        one hand. She looks out at the two of them, then at the
        window, then at the clock.)

NKECHI: Afternoon, Ada.

ADA:    Nkechi.

NKECHI: You've been here since three.

ADA:    I have.

NKECHI: You didn't have to be here since three.

ADA:    No.

        (Beat. NKECHI accepts this.)

NKECHI: (to TOBIAS) You're the seventeen-oh-five?

TOBIAS: Yes. Is it going?

NKECHI: Don't know yet.

TOBIAS: When will you know?

NKECHI: Sixteen fifty-five.

TOBIAS: Why sixteen fifty-five?

NKECHI: Because that's when I have to tell the crew, and I'm not telling them
        anything before I have to.

        (She closes the hatch.)

TOBIAS: (after a moment) That's not an answer.

ADA:    It's the answer.


        (Silence. Rain harder, then easing. TOBIAS stands, sits,
        stands.)

TOBIAS: I've got a thing at seven.

ADA:    Mm.

TOBIAS: On the other side. A thing at seven.

ADA:    There's a road.

TOBIAS: Fifty minutes.

ADA:    Fifty minutes gets you there at ten to six.

        (TOBIAS says nothing.)

ADA:    You could be in the car now.

TOBIAS: I know.

ADA:    You've known for an hour.

TOBIAS: I know.

        (Pause.)

TOBIAS: Why aren't you in a car?

ADA:    I don't drive.

TOBIAS: Ah.

ADA:    I did. I stopped in ninety-four.

TOBIAS: Why?

ADA:    Because I was seventy-eight and I could still tell.

        (TOBIAS laughs, surprised. It is the first easy sound in the
        room.)


TOBIAS: How long have you been doing this? Waiting.

ADA:    Which — today, or ---

TOBIAS: Generally.

ADA:    Sixty-one years.

TOBIAS: On this crossing?

ADA:    On this crossing. Twice a week, mostly.

TOBIAS: (calculating) That's ---

ADA:    Don't.

TOBIAS: Sorry.

ADA:    People always do the sum. It's not the sum. It's not six thousand
        crossings. It's one crossing, and I've done it a lot.

        (Pause.)

ADA:    You'll do it once and you'll remember today, and I've done it six
        thousand times and I'll remember about nine of them, and today won't
        be one.

TOBIAS: That's a bit ---

ADA:    It's not sad. It's the opposite of sad. It means it works.

        (Beat.)

ADA:    The nine I remember are the ones where it didn't.


        (The clock reads 16.53. TOBIAS is watching it. ADA is not.)

TOBIAS: Two minutes.

ADA:    Mm.

TOBIAS: Aren't you ---

ADA:    No.

TOBIAS: Why not?

ADA:    Because it makes no difference at all whether I'm looking at the
clock.

        (Pause. TOBIAS goes on looking at the clock. Then,
        deliberately, he turns his chair around to face away from it
        and sits.)

ADA:    (not looking) Better?

TOBIAS: No.

ADA:    Give it a minute.


        (16.55. The hatch opens. NKECHI comes out this time, through
        the side door, into the room. She stands. This is a
        different thing from speaking through a hatch and both women
        know it.)

NKECHI: It's off.

        (TOBIAS stands.)

TOBIAS: Off.

NKECHI: The seventeen-oh-five is cancelled. There's nothing after it tonight.

TOBIAS: But it might clear ---

NKECHI: It might.

TOBIAS: So why ---

NKECHI: Because I have to say now, and now I can't see the far bank.

        (Beat.)

NKECHI: If it clears at twenty past six I'll have been wrong. I'll know that
        at twenty past six. I don't know it now.

        (TOBIAS opens his mouth. Closes it.)

TOBIAS: Right.

NKECHI: The road's forty-eight minutes tonight. Nothing on it.

TOBIAS: Thank you.

        (He gathers his bag. Stops at the door.)

TOBIAS: For what it's worth — I'd rather you told me than ---

NKECHI: I know.

TOBIAS: I was going to say something else.

NKECHI: I know. Go on, you'll make your seven.

        (TOBIAS goes. The door closes. The two women are alone.)


        (NKECHI does not go back through the hatch. She sits down,
        one seat from ADA, in the front row, facing the fog.)

        (A long pause. Neither speaks. This should be allowed to
        last.)

ADA:    You'd have run it in ninety-four.

NKECHI: I wasn't here in ninety-four.

ADA:    Aurelio would have run it in ninety-four.

NKECHI: Aurelio ran a lot of things in ninety-four.

        (Beat.)

NKECHI: He wrote it all down. Eight pages. Left it on the desk.

ADA:    Did he.

NKECHI: Page six is about fog.

        (Pause.)

ADA:    What does page six say?

NKECHI: It says: you will be wrong both ways, and the ones you regret will be
        the ones where you went.

        (ADA nods slowly.)

ADA:    That sounds like him.

NKECHI: It's underlined.

        (Pause.)

ADA:    Is that why you didn't run it?

NKECHI: No.

        (Beat.)

NKECHI: It's why I'm sitting down.


        (Silence. The rain has stopped. Neither of them moves.)

        (Slowly — and this must be slow enough to be uncomfortable —
        the fog beyond the window begins to thin. Not clear: thin.
        Something that might be a far bank, or might not.)

        (ADA sees it. She does not say anything.)

        (NKECHI sees ADA seeing it.)

NKECHI: Don't.

ADA:    I wasn't going to.

        (They sit. The light behind the window continues, very
        gradually, to change.)

        (The clock reads 17.05.)


                            END OF SCENE


— This play is a work of fiction written as a sample asset. The terminal, the
crossing, and everyone in it are invented.
