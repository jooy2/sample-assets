FENWICK DISTRICT LIBRARIES
ANNUAL SERVICE REPORT

For the year ending 31 March 2027
Presented to the Culture and Communities Committee


SUMMARY

Eleven branches lent 1,284,600 items in the year to 31 March 2027, an increase of 4.1 per cent on the previous year and the third consecutive annual rise.
Membership stood at 61,340 at the year end, of whom 38,900 borrowed at least once during the year.

The service met eight of its eleven published targets.
It missed the target for average branch opening hours, missed the target for the proportion of stock acquired in the last five years, and missed the target for public computer availability at the Marlow Street branch, where the refurbishment overran by seven weeks.

This report sets out what the service did, what it cost, what it did not manage, and what the committee is asked to decide.

All figures in this report are invented for the purposes of a sample document.
They describe no real library service.


1. USE OF THE SERVICE

1.1 Lending

Loans rose in eight branches and fell in three.
The three that fell — Cooper Street, Halloway Bank, and the Tern Island mobile stop — are the three whose opening hours were reduced in the previous year's budget round.
The committee will draw its own conclusions; the service's view is that this is not a coincidence.

Loans by category:

    Adult fiction                412,300     (+2.1%)
    Adult non-fiction            208,900     (-1.4%)
    Children's                   341,700     (+9.8%)
    Audio and spoken word         96,400    (+14.2%)
    Large print                   71,200     (+3.0%)
    Periodicals                   84,100     (-6.7%)
    Other                         69,000     (+1.1%)

The rise in children's lending is the largest single movement in the report and is concentrated in the four branches that ran the summer reading programme.
The fall in periodicals continues a trend now eleven years old and is not thought to be reversible.

1.2 Visits

Door counts recorded 894,000 visits.
This figure should be treated as a ceiling rather than a measurement: the counters record a visitor who steps outside and returns as two visits, and no attempt has been made to correct for it.

More usefully, 38,900 members borrowed at least once, and 12,100 borrowed in nine months or more of the twelve.
That second group — a fifth of active members — accounts for 61 per cent of all loans.

1.3 Reference and enquiries

Staff logged 71,400 enquiries at service points.
The three commonest categories were: help with a government form (18 per cent), help with a device (16 per cent), and directions to somewhere that is not the library (9 per cent).

The service notes without comment that the second and third categories together outnumber enquiries about the stock.

1.4 Reading rooms and study space

Occupancy of study space averaged 47 per cent across opening hours, rising above 90 per cent in the four weeks before the summer examinations at three branches.
The service turned nobody away, but at Marlow Street on eleven occasions every seat was taken for more than two hours.


2. THE COLLECTION

2.1 Stock

The collection stood at 412,000 items at the year end, a net reduction of 6,200.
The service acquired 31,400 items and withdrew 37,600.

The withdrawal figure is higher than usual because the Halloway Bank branch was surveyed for the first time in nine years.
Of the 11,200 items on its shelves, 2,900 had not been borrowed since before the previous survey.
Of those, 2,100 were withdrawn and 800 were retained on the reference or local-history grounds set out in the stock policy.

2.2 Age of stock

The target is that 25 per cent of the lending collection should have been acquired within the last five years.
The figure achieved was 19.4 per cent, the fourth consecutive year below target.

The service does not regard this as a stock-selection failure.
Acquisition budgets have been flat in cash terms for six years while the average price of a hardback has risen 34 per cent.
The gap is arithmetic.

2.3 The Marlow Street bequest

The 212 volumes bequeathed in 2025 were catalogued in full during the year.
Conservation work is under way and is expected to complete in June, at which point the collection will be available to readers on the terms of the bequest.

The cataloguing threw up one item — a bound manuscript with no title page and an index in extremely poor Latin — which the cataloguer has described honestly as unidentifiable.
The service is content with that description and has declined a suggestion that it be replaced with something more confident.

Cost per loan was £4.86, down from £4.99. Cost per active member was £160.57.

Fine income fell 41 per cent following the removal of overdue charges on children's items in September.
The service regards this as money well not collected: borrowing by under-sixteens at the four branches with the highest previous fine income rose 22 per cent in the six months following.


4. WHAT DID NOT GO WELL

4.1 Opening hours

The target was an average of 41 hours per branch per week.
The achieved figure was 37.4.
Three branches lost hours in-year through vacancies that took longer than expected to fill.

The service draws the committee's attention to its own analysis, published in 2025 and unchallenged since, that opening hours predict borrowing considerably better than collection size does.
Hours are the variable most within the committee's control and the one most often reduced.

4.2 Marlow Street refurbishment

The refurbishment overran by seven weeks.
The cause was a floor void that proved to contain services not shown on any drawing held by the council, and the delay was not avoidable once discovered.
It was, however, foreseeable: the same discovery was made in the same building in 2011 and recorded in a file that nobody consulted.

Public computers at Marlow Street were unavailable for eleven weeks in total.
This is the largest single service failure in the year.

4.3 The mobile stop at Tern Island

The mobile library failed to reach Tern Island on nine of its twenty-six scheduled visits, in every case because the crossing was cancelled.
The service has no means of improving this and is discussing with the ferry cooperative whether a different day of the week would be less exposed.


5. WHAT THE COMMITTEE IS ASKED TO DECIDE

5.1 Restore the lost opening hours at Cooper Street, Halloway Bank, and the Tern Island stop, at an estimated cost of £71,000 in a full year.
    The service's view is that this is the single change most likely to raise use.

5.2 Increase the acquisitions budget by £90,000, which would return the proportion of stock under five years old to target within three years.

5.3 Agree that the eleven-branch network is reviewed in full during the coming year.
    Three branches are within 800 metres of another.
    The service is not recommending closures and is not opposed to them; it is asking that the question be settled deliberately rather than by attrition.

5.4 Note the conservation timetable for the Marlow Street bequest and the June deadline set by the terms of the gift.

APPENDIX B: A NOTE ON THE FIGURES

Loans are counted at issue, not at return, and a renewal counts as a loan.
Visits are door counts and are known to over-count.
Membership counts anybody holding a card that has not been dormant for three years.

The service has not changed any of these definitions during the year, which is worth stating because a report that changes its definitions and reports growth has reported nothing.
